# Randomised Trials of Digital Interventions Reporting Occupational Burnout Outcomes: A Scoping Review and Evidence Map

Short summary of the final results. Submission files are in [`submission/`](./submission/): blinded manuscript, title page, supplementary material, tracked-changes version, response to the pre-submission review, retrospective protocol and submission metadata.

## Method in brief

- **Reporting:** PRISMA-ScR with a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (databases plus registry-linked "other methods").
- **Sources:** Europe PMC, OpenAlex, ERIC (26 Sep 2026); Web of Science, Scopus (17 Sep 2026); supplementary search without the intervention-type block in Europe PMC and OpenAlex (28 Sep) and Web of Science and Scopus (29 Sep 2026); PubMed MeSH check and additional delivery terms in Europe PMC (29 Sep 2026); a unified update search (burnout AND any digital-delivery term AND randomised design) in Europe PMC, OpenAlex and PubMed on 18 Oct 2026 (the last search run); ClinicalTrials.gov linkage (28 Sep 2026).
- **Eligibility (applied in order):** primary results report, randomised allocation, working population, digital delivery, burnout outcome. The first failed criterion is the recorded reason.
- **Unit of analysis:** the trial. Reports sharing a registration or sample are one study.
- **Quality control:** a random sample of 45 records excluded in the main search contained no eligible report (exact 95% CI 0 to 7.9%); a blind sample of 30 reports was assessed by a human reviewer (agreement 80%, kappa 0.60); abstract-level coding was checked on a random 20% sample (23 trials). A unified Europe PMC search alone retrieved 81 of the 119 included reports (`data/unified_search_validation.csv`).

## Main findings (n = 116 trials)

- **Time:** 75 trials (64.7%) were first reported in 2023-2026; 17 before 2020.
- **Occupation:** healthcare workers 72 (other or mixed healthcare staff 35, physicians 22, nurses 15); mental-health and social care 7; teachers 15; employees in other sectors 22 (selected for a health condition or risk 9, named occupation or enterprise 5, mixed-occupation 8).
- **Mechanism:** psychological 96; non-psychological 20 (professional training 8, physical activity or health behaviour 6, feedback or navigation 3, workflow automation 3). Eleven of the 20 were found only by the searches after the main search.
- **Design:** waitlist 38 (40% of trials from 2023), active control 31, usual practice 27, head-to-head 8, not reported 12. Median 111 participants (IQR 66-237) in 107 trials with sample size.
- **Outcome reporting:** the burnout instrument is named in the abstract in 58 trials (50.0%). Full texts identified it for 55 of the other 58 (three not determined); the Maslach Burnout Inventory or an adaptation was used in 62 trials (53.4%).
- **Abstract-level reporting (not quality, not risk of bias):** a registration number was stated in 31 abstracts (26.7%), attrition or analysed numbers in 31 (26.7%), an analysis population in 17 (14.7%), blinding in 13 (11.2%) and a randomisation method in 7 (6.0%); "not stated" means not stated in the abstract. The abstract reported a burnout result favouring the intervention in 49 trials (42.2%), a mixed result in 27, no difference in 20, improvement in all arms in 4, an unfavourable result in 2 and no burnout result in 14 (`data/effect_direction_abstract.csv`; a description of reporting, not an effect estimate). On a random 20% sample (23 trials) the human reviewer agreed with the first coder on direction for 17 (kappa 0.62) and on 110 of 115 indicator judgements; all differences were resolved in favour of the human reviewer and the rules were applied to all trials.
- **Registry:** of 15 randomised registrations completed by 2023, 7 have a results report in this review, 2 published outside its scope and 6 (40%) have no located results report.

## Limitations

First-pass selection and charting by an AI assistant with a single human reviewer who checked samples; assessment mostly on abstracts; PsycINFO, Embase and CENTRAL not searched, no citation chasing, and the unified update search not repeated in Web of Science, Scopus or ERIC (each search added trials the previous one missed, so more probably remain); registry check limited to ClinicalTrials.gov; protocol not pre-registered; 2026 data incomplete.
