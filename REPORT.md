# Randomized Trials of Digital Interventions Reporting Occupational Burnout Outcomes: A Scoping Review and Evidence Map

Short summary of the final results. Full text: [`paper/burnout_scoping_review_paper.docx`](./paper/burnout_scoping_review_paper.docx).

## Method in brief

- **Reporting:** PRISMA-ScR with a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (databases plus registry-linked "other methods").
- **Sources:** Europe PMC, OpenAlex, ERIC (26 Sep 2026); Web of Science, Scopus (17 Sep 2026); a supplementary search without the intervention-type block in Europe PMC and OpenAlex (28 Sep 2026) and in Web of Science and Scopus (29 Sep 2026); a third supplementary search with a PubMed MeSH check and additional delivery terms in Europe PMC (29 Sep 2026); ClinicalTrials.gov linkage (28 Sep 2026).
- **Eligibility (applied in order):** primary results report, randomized allocation, working population, digital delivery, burnout outcome. The first failed criterion is the recorded reason.
- **Unit of analysis:** the trial. Reports sharing a registration or sample are one study.
- **Quality control:** every report passing screening was assessed against the criteria. A random sample of 45 records excluded in the main search contained no eligible report (exact 95% CI 0 to 7.9%). The three supplementary searches and the registry linkage found further eligible trials the main search had missed (27 trials from the supplementary searches).

## Main findings (n = 113 trials)

- **Time:** 73 trials (64.6%) were first reported in 2023–2026; 16 before 2020.
- **Occupation:** healthcare workers 70 (other or mixed healthcare staff 34, physicians 22, nurses 14); mental-health and social care 7; teachers 15; employees in other sectors 21 (selected for a health condition or risk 9, named occupation or enterprise 5, mixed-occupation 7).
- **Mechanism:** psychological 94; non-psychological 19 (professional training 8, physical activity or health behaviour 5, feedback or navigation 3, workflow automation 3). Ten of the 19 were found only by the supplementary searches.
- **Design:** waitlist 37 (40% of trials from 2023), active control 30, usual practice 27, head-to-head 8, not reported 11. Median 114.5 participants (IQR 69–238).
- **Outcome reporting:** the burnout instrument is named in the abstract in 56 trials (49.6%). Full texts identified it for 54 of the other 57 (three not determined); the Maslach Burnout Inventory or an adaptation was used in 60 trials (53.1%).
- **Abstract-level reporting (not quality, not risk of bias):** a registration number was stated in 35 abstracts (31.0%), attrition or completion numbers in 36 (31.9%), an analysis population in 20 (17.7%), blinding in 12 (10.6%) and a randomization method in 4 (3.5%); "not stated" means not stated in the abstract. The abstract reported a burnout result favouring the intervention in 56 trials (49.6%), no difference in 20, mixed results in 18, all arms improved in 3, an unfavourable result in 2 and no burnout result in 14 (`data/effect_direction_abstract.csv`; a description of reporting, not an effect estimate; a 20% sample awaits second-reviewer verification).
- **Registry:** of 15 randomized registrations completed by 2023, 7 have a results report in this review, 2 published outside its scope and 6 (40%) have no located results report.

## Limitations

Selection by one reviewer with an AI agent; a blind second reviewer assessed a sample of 30 reports (agreement 80%, kappa 0.60, 95% CI 0.31–0.89); assessment mostly on abstracts; PsycINFO, Embase and CENTRAL not searched, no citation chasing, and the third search limited to PubMed and Europe PMC (each search added trials the previous one missed, so more probably remain); registry check limited to ClinicalTrials.gov; protocol not pre-registered; 2026 data incomplete.
