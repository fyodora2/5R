# Randomised Trials of Digital Interventions Reporting Occupational Burnout Outcomes: A Scoping Review and Evidence Map

Short summary of the final results. Submission files are in [`submission/`](./submission/): blinded manuscript, title page, supplementary material, tracked-changes version, response to the pre-submission review, retrospective protocol and submission metadata.

## Method in brief

- **Reporting:** PRISMA-ScR with a PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (databases plus registry-linked "other methods").
- **Sources:** Europe PMC, OpenAlex, ERIC (26 Sep 2026); Web of Science, Scopus (17 Sep 2026); supplementary search without the intervention-type block in Europe PMC and OpenAlex (28 Sep) and Web of Science and Scopus (29 Sep 2026); PubMed MeSH check and additional delivery terms in Europe PMC (29 Sep 2026); a unified update search (burnout AND any digital-delivery term AND randomised design) in Europe PMC, OpenAlex, PubMed and ERIC on 18 Oct 2026 (the last search run); ClinicalTrials.gov linkage (28 Sep 2026) with later searches for the publications of unlocated registrations; citation chasing of five reviews.
- **Eligibility (applied in order):** primary results report, randomised allocation, working population, digital delivery, burnout outcome (any aim, as an outcome of the randomised comparison). The first failed criterion is the recorded reason.
- **Unit of analysis:** the trial. Reports sharing a registration or sample are one study.
- **Quality control:** a random sample of 45 records excluded in the main search contained no eligible report (exact 95% CI 0 to 7.9%); a blind sample of 30 reports was assessed by a human reviewer (agreement 80%, kappa 0.60); abstract-level coding was checked on a random 20% sample (23 trials). A unified Europe PMC search alone retrieved 81 of the 121 included reports (`data/unified_search_validation.csv`).

## Main findings (n = 117 trials)

- **Time:** 76 trials (65.0%) were first reported in 2023-2026; 16 before 2020.
- **Occupation:** healthcare workers 73 (other or mixed healthcare staff 35, physicians 22, nurses 16); mental-health and social care 7; teachers 16; employees in other sectors 21.
- **Mechanism:** psychological 96; non-psychological 21 (professional training 9, physical activity or health behaviour 6, feedback or navigation 3, workflow automation 3).
- **Design:** waitlist 38 (40.8% of trials from 2023), active control 32, usual practice 27, head-to-head 8, not reported 12.
- **Outcome reporting:** the burnout instrument is named in the abstract in 58 trials (49.6%); full texts identified it for 57 of the other 59 (two not determined); the Maslach Burnout Inventory or an adaptation was used in 64 trials.
- **Abstract-level reporting (not quality, not risk of bias):** registration number in 31 abstracts, attrition or analysed numbers in 32, analysis population in 17, blinding in 13, randomisation method in 7. Direction of the burnout result in the abstract: favours the intervention 50, mixed 27, no difference 20, all arms improved 4, unfavourable 2, no burnout result 14 (`data/effect_direction_abstract.csv`; reporting description, not effect estimate). On the 23-trial verification sample the human reviewer agreed with the first coder on direction for 17 (kappa 0.62) and on 110 of 115 indicator judgements.
- **Registry:** of 15 randomised registrations completed by 2023, 9 have a results report in this review, 2 published outside its scope and 4 (27%) have no located results report.

## Limitations

First-pass selection and charting by an AI assistant with a single human reviewer who checked samples; assessment mostly on abstracts (trials measuring burnout only in the full text can be missed); PsycINFO, Embase and CENTRAL not searched; the unified update search not repeated in Web of Science or Scopus (each search added trials the previous one missed, so more probably remain); registry check limited to ClinicalTrials.gov; protocol not pre-registered; 2026 data incomplete.
