Scoping Review & Evidence Map · Europe PMC + OpenAlex + ERIC + Web of Science + Scopus · computational first pass

Randomized Digital Interventions Reporting Occupational Burnout Outcomes

145 randomized-trial reports (a current maximum of ~143 independent trials) from five databases, coded across six dimensions, semantically clustered, and mapped over 17 years of publication. The corpus went through several documented rounds of post-hoc screening-validation and Scopus title-resolution correction (139→137→138→144→145) -- see the Methods tab. See the Methods tab for screening criteria and limitations before treating this as publication-ready.

Search & screening — five databases

Europe PMC (field TITLE_ABS, four AND'd concept groups): 227 raw → 213 unique → automated screening on MEDLINE pubType + design language + occupational-population terms → 84 include / 32 uncertain / 97 exclude. (85 initially included; 1 was reclassified as excluded/merged on post-hoc audit — a duplicate SSRN preprint of an already-included published trial, PsyCovidApp — see screening-validation note below.)

OpenAlex (same query, personal API key, title_and_abstract.search): 716 raw → 315 not already found via Europe PMC → 46 automated include → manual reconciliation (removed conference-abstract companion reports, a protocol, a mis-screened systematic-review registration, internal duplicate indexing) → 30 initially included → 29 new studies (1, "GRIT-J", was reclassified as excluded on post-hoc audit — an unpublished OSF trial protocol/registration, not a completed-trial results report).

ERIC (tests whether teacher-focused studies were an Europe-PMC-specific gap): 8 candidates → 7 excluded (2 in-person CARE-for-Teachers reports with no digital component, 1 protocol/goals paper with no results, 1 student-only population, 1 non-intervention conceptual paper, 1 unrelated conference proceedings, 1 self-taught-vs-instructor-led format comparison with no digital-vs-control contrast) → 1 new study.

Web of Science (official export, own institutional access, same four-concept query on the Topic field): 352 raw → 274 not already found → 34 automated include → manual reconciliation excluded 14 trial protocols (a systematic issue: protocol abstracts describe a planned control arm in future tense, which satisfies the same regex a completed trial does) plus 2 false positives → 18 new studies.

Scopus (own institutional access; the official bulk export was unavailable, so only a title/year/source capture of the visible results — no abstracts — could be retrieved): 382 raw → 284 not already found. Since Scopus provided no abstract, resolution was attempted in four steps: (1) 201/284 resolved against OpenAlex by title match, screened (120 excluded, 57 uncertain), manual reconciliation of 24 auto-included/resolved-uncertain kept 5; (2) 1/284 with no abstract anywhere was resolved via its ClinicalTrials.gov registration and kept; (3) of the 83 still-unresolved titles, a follow-up Crossref DOI lookup matched 57 to a DOI (38 with a usable abstract), and screening + reconciliation of those 38 found 1 further eligible study; (4) of the remaining 44 titles, an extended full-text search (Europe PMC, publisher pages, and a full re-check of Crossref/Semantic Scholar metadata for those without a usable abstract) reached an independently-verified decision for every one of the 44, across two passes: 7 were eligible (6 found in an initial extended search, 1 more on a full re-examination of the remaining titles prompted by a second pre-submission review), 1 was a duplicate of an already-included Europe PMC report (a small cross-database dedup gap, not a new study), and the other 36 were excluded for a documented reason (6 wrong population, 11 non-randomized/observational design, 4 study protocol or correction/erratum notice, 3 systematic review rather than a primary study, 2 non-digital or unconfirmed-digital delivery, 8 off-topic, 2 excluded conservatively because full text could not be accessed to confirm eligibility). Net: 13 new studies; no Scopus titles remain unresolved — the full per-title table (DOI where available, decision, reason) is in Appendix A of the manuscript.

A supplementary ClinicalTrials.gov registry-matching check, using a reproducible filter (Condition contains "Burnout" + Interventional + a digital keyword in the title/intervention + non-occupational-population titles excluded), found 28 matched completed trials (full list with NCT IDs, completion dates, and match decisions in Appendix C of the manuscript). A title-Jaccard similarity match was tried first but proved unreliable (registry titles often differ substantially from eventual article titles), so targeted intervention-name/acronym search was used instead, with each match manually confirmed for population/mechanism fit. Only 8 of 28 (29%) have a confirmed matching publication in this corpus — a registry-to-publication matching result, not a definitive publication-bias estimate (the search method itself can miss real publications). This is a supplementary check, not folded into the corpus.

Screening validation (two separate, independently-timed checks — not one combined sample): (a) two independent random samples (n=15 each, fixed seeds) were manually reviewed against the eligibility criteria. All 15 sampled Europe PMC exclusions were confirmed correct (this estimates Europe PMC's false-negative rate only, not all five databases). The 15 sampled inclusions (all five databases) were drawn from the 138-record corpus stage (i.e., after the PsyCovidApp duplicate below had already been removed, but before this same sample surfaced the next issue): 11 were clearly eligible, 1 was a confirmed false positive ("GRIT-J" — an OSF trial protocol written entirely in future tense, not a completed-trial report — removed, corpus → 137), and 3 were borderline-but-retained under the documented eligibility operationalization (a hybrid yoga trial with a non-exclusive digital homework component, a single-arm sub-analysis nested in a larger RCT, and a weight-loss eHealth trial that measured burnout only as a secondary outcome). (b) Separately, and independently of that 15-record sample, a systematic pairwise title-similarity scan (Jaccard>0.55) across the full original 139-record corpus caught one further true duplicate: the PsyCovidApp SSRN preprint vs. its published JMIR version (Jaccard=0.84) — removed. Combined effect of (a)+(b): 139 → 137. Two further Scopus abstract-recovery steps (Crossref, then an extended full-text search) brought the corpus to 144. A second pre-submission review then prompted a full re-examination of every remaining Scopus title rather than a partial pass, which found one further eligible study that had been present in the Crossref-resolved-but-abstract-missing bucket but overlooked in that first extended search, bringing the corpus to 145. A fourth borderline case (an app-based exercise RCT recovered via Crossref, whose mechanism is behavioral/physiological rather than classically "psychological") is discussed in the manuscript's §4.3.

Data handling note: Web of Science and Scopus license terms restrict bulk redistribution of their records. This dashboard and the companion repository therefore keep title/year/DOI/journal and this project's own coded tags for those 30 studies, but not their original abstract text.

Limitations

No PsycINFO or Cochrane CENTRAL access from this environment, and citation chasing of included studies was not performed — so apparent under-representation (e.g. corporate/office employees) reflects the retrieved corpus, not a proven research gap. Screening included one manual reconciliation pass per database beyond Europe PMC, not a full independent dual-human review; the screening-validation sample above covers only ~2.7% of the 1,094 screened records, so similar undetected errors may remain. All screening/coding decisions were made from titles/abstracts, never systematic full-text retrieval. Coding is abstract-level only (or, for one Scopus record, a trial-registry synopsis), so technology/instrument/comparator fields are frequently "unspecified" — meaning not mentioned in the abstract, not necessarily absent from the study. Approach/technology charts use multi-label coding (bar heights and cell counts are label counts, not unique-study counts). Clustering silhouette is low (0.009), reflecting a topically homogeneous corpus — read cluster themes qualitatively/exploratorily, not as a valid statistical partition; cluster-vs-seed/k stability was not tested. Several studies are companion reports of the same underlying trial (e.g. three WISER-trial publications); the "~143 independent trials" estimate (145 minus 2 extra WISER reports) is a current ceiling on trials identified so far, not a lower bound — further undetected multi-report trials would only reduce this number. Eligibility criteria accept burnout as a secondary outcome and non-classically-psychological mechanisms (e.g. exercise) that report burnout as an outcome; four documented borderline cases are listed above and in the manuscript, with a sensitivity check (n=145 vs. n=141) showing top occupation/approach rankings are unaffected. No meta-analysis: this is an evidence map.

Reproducing this

Full pipeline (fetch → dedupe/screen → code → cluster → analyze, per database, plus the merge step) and every intermediate dataset ship in the companion repository's scripts/ and data/ folders, with the full write-up in REPORT.md. Web of Science and Scopus raw exports are not included in the repository (see data-handling note above); only this project's derived, non-abstract data is.

Data: Europe PMC, OpenAlex, ERIC (retrieved 2026-09-26), plus Web of Science and Scopus (institutional access, retrieved 2026-09-27; abstracts not redistributed here per license terms). Pipeline: Python (urllib, scikit-learn TF-IDF/KMeans). This dashboard is a computational first pass intended to scope a full scoping-review protocol, not a finished systematic evidence synthesis.
